Negative result · single-GPU architecture study

A single neuron is deep. That does not make it a better language model.

Implementing the dendritic NMDA mechanism faithfully made the model 0.200 nats worse — 27 σ — and the network never learned to switch it off.

RTX 4070 · 12 GB d=512 · 8 layers · ctx 1024 FineWeb-Edu · 100M tokens/run 4 + 3 seeds

Recent work shows a single human cortical neuron needs a ~7-layer artificial network to emulate, driven by nonlinear NMDA conductances in its dendrites. A tempting engineering inference follows: build networks from more sophisticated neurons rather than more of them. This is a compute-matched test of that inference at 50M parameters. It fails, and the direction of failure is unambiguous.

01 / ResultThe more faithfully the biology is implemented, the worse the model

Every arm sees byte-identical tokens in byte-identical order. Only the feedforward unit differs. μ is the strength of the multiplicative NMDA coincidence term — the mechanism the neuroscience identifies as the source of a neuron's computational depth.

Two independent settings of μ give the same monotone ordering: more multiplication is much worse, less is less bad, none at all is best. The hypothesis is not merely unsupported — it is inverted.

02 / Mechanismμ affects quality strongly and receives no useful gradient

Initialised at 1.0, μ finished training at 0.94–1.02 across every layer and tree level (std ≈ 0.03). The optimizer had 100M tokens to notice that μ was costing it 0.2 nats and dial it toward zero. It did not move it at all.

From the other end, μ initialised at 0.0 random-walked up to ~0.02, following mean|μ| = 0.00153·√step — the signature of gradient noise, not learning.

The network re-equilibrates every other parameter around whatever μ it is given, settling into a worse optimum with a locally flat gradient in μ. It stays stuck wherever it is put.

This generalises past dendrites. An architecture knob that materially determines final quality while receiving no useful gradient is a trap — it looks tuned, and it is not.

03 / DepthDepth inside the unit does not substitute for depth in the network

The headline hypothesis was that a 4-layer dendritic model could match a 16-layer transformer, because network depth is sequential — the latency floor of every forward pass — while a dendritic tree is internally parallel. Measured against a dense baseline depth curve, the depth-compression ratio ρ is 0.83 (0.64–0.94 depending on interpolation). The hypothesis needed ρ ≥ 4.

04 / InstrumentAll 16 sub-blocks earn their keep — and the cheap proxy says otherwise

Counting layers is not measuring them. We built an instrument with two independent readouts per sub-block: a geometric one — input/output cosine and relative update norm — and a causal one, lesioning each sub-block and measuring the validation-loss increase.

On the 8-layer baseline, the geometric readout looks damning: cosine 0.92–0.98 for every block after the first, which reads as "these layers barely change anything." The causal readout disagrees completely. Lesion deltas run 0.070 to 4.53 nats, every one far above the 0.0058 threshold at which a sub-block earns what an extra layer buys.

Effective depth: 16 / 16 sub-blocks. No dead depth at this scale — and the cheap geometric proxy would have told us the opposite.

This falsified our own follow-up hypothesis before it cost a single GPU-hour. We had planned an intervention to reclaim "wasted" nominal depth; there is none to reclaim at 50M parameters. It also means a high input/output correlation is not evidence that a layer is doing nothing — a small update can be well-aimed and load-bearing.

05 / CorrectionsThe first version of this experiment was wrong, and every defect flattered it

An adversarial audit found that the original run never activated its own mechanism. The defects are listed in full because they are the most useful part of the record.

  1. F1The mechanism never ran μ initialised at exactly 0 and never left gradient noise, so a nominally degree-2³ multiplicative unit trained as a flat additive tree — algebraically the published null result it existed to move beyond. Nominal unit depth 5; measured junction correlation 0.99. The independent variable was never varied.
  2. F2A rounding bug biased every run against the new architecture The FLOP matcher bisected a continuous variable then snapped to a grid, so it never minimised |flops − target|. Every dendritic run took −2.15% FLOPs and −3.8% params when a strictly closer grid point existed: roughly 12% of the measured gap. The repo's own matching assertion would have caught it and was never called.
  3. F3An exciting finding that was noise A 2M-token smoke test showed the new unit ahead by 0.19 nats. It sat on the wrong side of a crossover. The crossover was then oversold: the published table subsampled every third eval and omitted step 0, where the unit was already behind, and the "ahead" region's scatter was the size of the effect. Its FFN output std at init was 5.2× the baseline's, which alone predicts faster early progress.
  4. F4Single-seed conclusions Everything rested on 0.03-nat differences with no measured σ. The true baseline σ is 0.0020; the dendritic arm's is 0.0101 — 5× larger. Both must be measured, since the comparison is a difference of two random variables.
  5. F5A log filter that could only show good news Training was piped into grep -E "^step|^FINAL". A Python traceback matches neither pattern, so seven consecutive out-of-memory crashes appeared as silent completions.
  6. F6Sixteen trainings, zero checkpoints saved F1 could therefore only be found by retraining from scratch, and no post-hoc analysis of any run was possible.
Every one of these produced a more publishable-looking result than the truth. The failure modes were not random — they were biased toward apparent success.

A 2026 multi-seed reproduction study finds most published transformer modifications land inside 3-seed noise, several well-known ones negative on re-testing. This is a worked example of how that happens without anyone intending it.

06 / ReadingWhat the biology claim survives

The neuroscience is not in dispute. A human cortical neuron plausibly does require a deep network to emulate, and NMDA nonlinearity plausibly is why. What fails is the engineering inference drawn from it.

A consistent reading across the literature: intra-neuron multiplicative depth does not buy language-model quality (this work); compute-matched dendrites give no expressivity gain (Wu et al., Patterns 2026); and the transformer-like computation in biology may live in the neuron–astrocyte loop rather than the dendritic tree (Kozachkov, Kastanenka & Krotov, PNAS 2023).

Biology's per-unit depth is plausibly an answer to wiring cost — space, energy, slow axons — a constraint a GPU does not have. Evolution was solving a different problem.

07 / LimitsWhat this does not show

  • μ = 1.0 is one setting. Both measured endpoints point toward zero, but a small optimum near μ ≈ 0.1 is not excluded. What is excluded is that substantial multiplicativity helps.
  • One scale and one budget — d=512, 100M tokens, well short of compute-optimal.
  • One tree topology — reducing butterfly, 3 levels. The tree-depth sweep was cut.
  • Wall-clock is not comparable across arms — gradient checkpointing was on for the dendritic unit only, and the first baseline ran on a cold GPU. ρ is measured in layers, not seconds, so it is unaffected.
  • The no-tree, flat and frozen-μ controls are single-seed.

08 / Also measuredTwo side findings

The bandwidth "win" was a capacity cost

The design sold a 3.2× reduction in activation traffic into the output projection as elegance: 5632 branches funnelled into 704 somas, where the baseline writes from 2048. Measured, the no-tree variant — same unit, no width reduction — is the best of the dendritic family and recovers roughly half the gap. The narrow port was mostly just a narrow port.

Noise floor at this scale

Baseline σ = 0.0020 over 4 seeds at 100M tokens, total range 0.0049. Most architecture effects reported at this scale are smaller than that, and therefore unmeasurable without multiple seeds.
